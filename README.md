# perseverance_efficiency
Data analysis project analyzing how much extra distance Perseverance covers compared to driving in a straight line, and what determining predicts it. Received 2nd in ThinkNeuro SWE Summer 2026 Cohort, presenting at SSCUR on November 21st, 2026.

Abstract:
Perseverance is a Mars Rover designed by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) equipped with AutoNav: a system designed for Mars Rovers to avoid planetary hazards and navigate between two points autonomously. Perseverance uses nuclear energy, a declining power source that produces energy at a rate similar to what a standard lightbulb consumes—causing NASA engineers to place heavy emphasis on energy efficiency. This raises the question: if NASA is concerned about energy limitations on Mars yet has not publicly characterized the detour ratio of AutoNav on Perseverance, is AutoNav’s potential excess distance an uncharacterized source of excess energy consumption, considering energy consumption scales with distance? This study utilizes NASA’s M20 dataset to calculate the detour ratio of Perseverance. Data were grouped and filtered to 464 drives and matched with calculated displacement and quotient of path length and displacement: detour ratio. Modeling drive distance as a linear function of displacement separates two distinct components of the excess travel: a fixed per-drive cost of 4.6 meters and a proportional cost of roughly 9.4% of each meter traveled, with a 0.95 R2 value. Ultimately, this study characterizes the detour ratio of AutoNav: approximately one quarter of excess distance reflects large-scale course correction rather than local obstacle avoidance—indicating a measurable cost of autonomous path selection.
